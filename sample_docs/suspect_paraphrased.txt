City parks and gardens are essential for making urban life better. When cities have more green areas, the air becomes cleaner because plants filter out harmful pollutants. These spaces also give people a place to stay active, which leads to improved health across the population. On top of that, wildlife such as birds and small animals benefit from the natural habitats that parks provide, helping local ecosystems thrive. Communities with well-developed public parks often experience closer social bonds, since shared outdoor spaces bring neighbors together naturally. Trees also cool down cities by offering shade, which helps counter the higher temperatures typically found in heavily built urban zones. As a result, modern city planners now see green spaces as a fundamental part of building sustainable cities, not just a nice extra.
