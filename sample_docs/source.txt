Urban green spaces play a critical role in improving the quality of life in cities. Parks and gardens reduce air pollution by filtering harmful particles from the atmosphere. They also provide residents with areas for physical exercise, which contributes to better public health outcomes. Beyond health benefits, green spaces support local biodiversity by offering habitats for birds, insects, and small mammals. Cities that invest in expanding their parks tend to see stronger community engagement, as public spaces naturally encourage social interaction among neighbors. Additionally, trees and vegetation in green spaces help regulate urban temperatures by providing shade and reducing the heat-island effect common in densely built areas. For these reasons, urban planners increasingly treat green space development as a core component of sustainable city design rather than an optional amenity.
